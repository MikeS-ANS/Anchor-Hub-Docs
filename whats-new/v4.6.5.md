# v4.6.5 — Payroll Processing: due-day reminders, deadline visibility, and Pay Calendar

Released 22 September 2026.

Everything below is in **Payroll Processing**. Nothing else in the Hub changed.

## A reminder on the deadline day itself

Managers used to get a heads-up two business days before their sign-off deadline, then nothing else until the deadline had already passed and the overdue nagging started — a silent day in between. A Teams reminder now goes out at **9:00 a.m. on the deadline itself**, once per manager, to anyone who still hasn't signed off.

## The deadline is visible before it's missed

The sign-off deadline used to show up only in the red past-due banner — after it was already too late. It now shows on your own **My People** screen from the start, right alongside the period dates and pay date, with **"(due today)"** added once it arrives and **"(past due)"** if it's missed. Heather and Mike see the same date on the **Review & Send** screen.

## Pay Calendar — planning a year of deadlines in advance

A new tab, **Pay Calendar** (Mike and Heather only), lists the **next 24 pay periods — a full year out** — with each one's sign-off deadline. It exists because the Hub only knows about weekends, not company holidays, so a deadline could land squarely on Thanksgiving or Christmas Eve and the reminders would go out on schedule anyway, nagging right through it.

From this tab, a deadline can be adjusted well ahead of time. Weekend deadlines are badged so they're easy to spot at a glance; a row that's been changed shows who changed it and when; and moving the deadline on an already-open period re-sends its reminders, so a manager who was told the old date gets told the new one too, instead of quietly working toward a date that no longer applies. A period already sent to Puzzle is locked — its deadline is history at that point.

## The entry window opens later, not on day one

Managers can now only enter changes starting **three business days before their sign-off deadline**, instead of three calendar days after the period started. The old timing meant a manager could be asked to log on-call hours and bonuses for work that hadn't happened yet. Until the window opens, the screen names the period and the date you'll be able to start, instead of just showing an empty grid.

## A closed period no longer sits on top of the next one

Pay periods run back-to-back, so for the last three days of every cycle, an already-sent period used to keep sitting in front of the newly-opened one — and because the manager screen has no period picker, that was a real lockout, not just a mild annoyance. A sent period now steps out of the way as soon as it's sent, so the next one is there waiting for you.
