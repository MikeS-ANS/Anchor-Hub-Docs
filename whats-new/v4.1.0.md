# v4.1.0 — Daily Progress

Released 4 September 2026.

## Daily Progress

A new tool, and a personal one: an end-of-day record of what you actually moved forward, built from your own Sent Items, calendar, Autotask ticket work, and Teams chats. It was Cory Pottratz's idea.

Open it from the sidebar and today's report generates on the spot. You get:

- **Four tiles** — actions taken, clients touched, meetings, and hours logged. Once you have a few days saved, each one also shows what's typical for you and a small sparkline, so a number has something to be read against.
- **What actually moved forward** — your sent-mail threads, the Autotask tickets you worked, and the meetings that already happened, in one timeline, earliest first.
- **Teams chats** — a card of their own, listing who you talked with and how many messages went each way, busiest conversation first.
- **Meetings** — the day's whole calendar, including what hasn't happened yet.
- **This week** — the same picture across the week, with each Teams conversation merged across the days it ran.
- **Email me** — sends the report to your own mailbox, with a standalone copy attached that opens in any browser.
- **An opt-in scheduler** — generates the day's report at a time you pick, optionally emails it to you, and catches up the next time you launch the Hub if your machine was off. It never generates a report *for* a Saturday or Sunday, though it will still fill in the previous workday if that one was missed.

Every past day is browsable, and you can generate one for a day you missed.

### Why the Teams card has no times

A chat isn't an event that happened at a moment — it runs across the whole day. An early version put each chat in the timeline stamped with the first thing you said in it, which read as *"this is when you two talked"* when it was only when the conversation started. So chats moved out of the timeline into their own card, and the time came off entirely. What's left is what stays true all day: who, and how many messages each way.

### What it reads — and what it doesn't

Only your own Sent Items, your own calendar, your own Autotask work, and your own Teams chats.

Not your inbox. Not Teams channels. Not the text of any Teams message. Nothing belonging to anyone else.

Each day's report is saved to **your** OneDrive, in `Apps/Anchor Hub` — not to a shared database. One AI-written line per sent-email thread is the single thing that leaves Microsoft: Hatz.ai writes it from the text *you* sent, and it's screened for money, pay and compensation terms, and identifying detail before it is ever shown to you — if it trips any of those, you get the subject line instead. Everything else on the page is measured rather than generated, and the page tells you which is which.

**There is no manager view, no team rollup, and no comparison with anyone else — at any layer, including the database.** A first-run screen spells all of this out before your first report is ever generated, and re-appears once if that answer ever changes.

## Sidebar Layout

Settings → **Customize** is now its own entry in the sidebar, called **Sidebar Layout**, directly under Ideas & Bugs. It's the same screen with the same buttons — it just isn't buried inside Settings any more.

If you have written instructions or a bookmark that says "Settings → Customize," that's this.
